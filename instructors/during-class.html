During class: see and help learners

What you can see in Monitor Labs, how to message a learner, take control, share files, and turn labs on or off.

Audience
Instructors and TAs
Time
8 min read

Quick reference

When you need to… Do this
See who’s working, and on what Class page, then Monitor Labs
Spot someone who has stepped away In Monitor Labs, sort by time since last activity
Watch a learner’s screen Monitor Labs, then Monitor Student
Fix something together Send them a message, then Take Control
Give everyone a file Monitor Labs, then Instructor Files, then Share Files
Stop learners launching the wrong lab Class page, untick Enabled on that activity
Add a learner who isn’t on the roster Send their name and email to the CSM
Fix a broken lab When something goes wrong

Open Monitor Labs

  1. Open your class. Select My Calendar, then the class name. Or use My Classes.
  2. Select Monitor Labs. It opens in a new browser tab. Keep it open for the whole session.

What you can see

Monitor Labs lists every lab that is running right now in your class. For each one you see:

  • The learner and the lab they’re in
  • The active virtual machine
  • How long the lab has been running
  • Time since the learner’s last activity

Switch the Layout between small, medium or large tiles, or a list, to fit your whole class on one screen.

What you can’t see here: learners who haven’t launched a lab yet, or whose lab is saved and closed, won’t appear. Nothing outside the lab, such as the learner’s own computer or their GitHub account, is visible to you. If you need to know whether someone launched a lab at all, ask the CSM, who can look it up in TMS.

Message a learner

Use Send Notifications on the Monitor Labs page to send a learner a message. It’s the polite way to say “I’m going to take control for a minute” before you do.

Watch or take control

From Monitor Labs, find the learner, then choose:

  • Monitor Student: watch their lab screen as they work. They keep control.
  • Take Control: work directly in their lab to fix something together.

Etiquette: tell the learner before you take control, narrate what you’re doing, and hand control back explicitly.

On some lab types only one connection works at a time. If both screens flicker, ask the learner to close their lab window while you work. The lab keeps running, and they rejoin with Launch from their class page.

Share a file with the class

In Monitor Labs, use Instructor Files, then Share Files. A pop-up appears in every learner’s running lab to say a file is ready. Each file must be under 200 MB. To leave a file on the class page for later instead, use Manage Files on the Class page and make sure Students is ticked under Visibility.

Turn a lab on or off for the class

If the course allows it, each activity on the Class page has an Enabled checkbox. Untick it to stop learners launching that lab (they see it greyed out), and tick it again to give access back. Changes reach learners within about 30 seconds. This is how you keep learners on today’s lab. If you don’t see the checkboxes, ask the lab admin.

Your session rhythm

Start of session

  • Open Monitor Labs in a tab.
  • Name the exact lab to launch.
  • Tell learners how long the lab runs, and show the start-up routine: clone or pull, sign in, set keys.

During

  • Check in on anyone idle for a long time. They may be heading for an idle timeout.

15 minutes before the end

  • “Commit, push, and check it’s on GitHub.”
  • Then Save and Close or End, as the program specifies.
